## Heat Range Guide

**Understanding heat range numbers:**
- **Lower number** = HOTTER plug (retains more heat)
- **Higher number** = COLDER plug (dissipates heat faster)

**When to go COLDER (higher number):**
- Turbocharged/supercharged engines under high boost
- Performance tuning (higher compression, more aggressive timing)
- Extended high-RPM use (track days)
- Hot climate operation

**When to stay STOCK heat range:**
- Street-driven vehicles
- Factory tune
- Daily drivers
- Most applications

**When to go HOTTER (lower number):**
- Extremely cold climates (rare)
- Low-load operation only (not recommended for most cars)

**Example:**
- BMW N52 stock: NGK heat range 6
- N52 supercharged: NGK heat range 7 (one step colder)

## Gap Settings by Application

| Engine Type | Gap Range | Notes |
|-------------|-----------|-------|
| Naturally aspirated (port injection) | 0.040-0.055" | Wider gap = stronger spark |
| Turbocharged (port injection) | 0.028-0.035" | Narrower gap = reliable spark under boost |
| Direct injection | 0.032-0.043" | Tighter tolerance |
| High-performance/tuned | 0.024-0.032" | Tighter gap for high boost |

**CRITICAL:**
- Modern iridium/platinum plugs often come pre-gapped - CHECK before installing
- Never force-gap iridium plugs (can crack electrode)
- Use proper gapping tool (wire-type gauge, not ramp gauge)

## Installation Torque Specifications

**By thread size:**
- **M12 (most common)**: 15-18 ft-lb
- **M14**: 18-22 ft-lb
- **M18 (large trucks)**: 22-30 ft-lb

**By head material:**
- **Cast iron heads**: Use upper torque range
- **Aluminum heads**: Use lower torque range (over-torquing cracks threads)

**With crush washer:**
- Tighten until washer compresses (usually around spec torque)
- Don't reuse old washers

**Tapered seat (no washer):**
- Hand-tighten, then 1/16 turn with wrench
- More delicate - don't over-torque

## Common Mistakes

1. **Using wrong heat range**
   - Too cold: Fouling, poor cold starts
   - Too hot: Pre-ignition, detonation, melted electrodes

2. **Incorrect gap**
   - Too wide on turbo engines = misfires under boost
   - Too narrow = weak spark, poor combustion

3. **Over-torquing**
   - Aluminum heads strip easily
   - Stuck plugs impossible to remove

4. **Mixing brands/types in same engine**
   - Use all same brand and model
   - Inconsistent combustion otherwise

5. **Not using anti-seize on aluminum heads**
   - BMW, Honda aluminum heads benefit from anti-seize on threads
   - Don't use on Ford (Ford says no anti-seize)
