# Workshop Standards and Default Assumptions

## Purpose
This document defines default standards and assumptions for the workshop. These rules are applied automatically unless customer specifies otherwise or specific vehicle requirements dictate different choices.

---

## Parts Quality Standards

### **Safety-Critical Components - OEM ONLY**
**Never compromise on safety. Always use OEM or OEM-equivalent parts.**

**Mandatory OEM parts:**
- Brake components (pads, rotors, calipers, hydraulics)
- Suspension components (control arms, ball joints, tie rods)
- Airbag system components
- Steering components
- ABS components
- Seatbelt components

**Why:** Aftermarket safety parts have inconsistent quality. Customer safety is non-negotiable.

**Exception:** High-quality branded aftermarket (Brembo brakes, Bilstein suspension) acceptable if equal or better than OEM.

---

### **Engine Components - OEM Preferred**

**Use OEM or OEM-supplier brands:**
- Timing belts/chains
- Head gaskets
- Engine seals
- Water pumps (especially timing belt driven)
- Thermostats
- Engine mounts

**Acceptable aftermarket brands:**
- Gates, Dayco (timing components)
- Fel-Pro (gaskets)
- Aisin (OEM supplier for Toyota/others)
- Bosch (ignition, sensors)

**Avoid:**
- Unknown Chinese brands
- Ultra-budget parts from discount suppliers
- Parts without brand name/markings

---

### **Wear Items - Quality Aftermarket Acceptable**

**Customer choice based on budget:**
- Oil filters (but recommend quality brands)
- Air filters
- Cabin filters
- Wiper blades
- Light bulbs

**Our default brands:**
- Oil filters: OEM, Purolator, Wix, Mann, Mahle
- Air filters: OEM, K&N, Fram Ultra (not regular Fram)
- Cabin filters: OEM or any quality brand

---

### **Fluids - OEM Spec Required, Brand Flexible**

**Follow OEM specifications, not necessarily OEM brand:**

**Engine Oil:**
- **BMW LL-01 required** → Use Castrol Edge Professional, Mobil 1 FS, or BMW brand
- **VW 502.00 required** → Use approved oils (Castrol, Mobil 1, Liqui Moly)
- **Dexos required (GM)** → Use Dexos-certified oil
- **General vehicles** → Full synthetic if turbo, 5W-30 or 5W-20 typical

**Default oil change interval:** 5,000-7,500 miles (ignore OEM "10k mile" intervals for longevity)

**Transmission Fluid:**
- Use OEM-specified type (Dexron VI, Mercon LV, VW G 052 162, etc.)
- **Never use "universal" ATF** on European cars

**Coolant:**
- BMW: Blue coolant (nitrite-free)
- VW/Audi: G12/G13 (pink/purple)
- Honda: Blue Type 2
- Toyota: Red Long Life
- **Never mix coolant types**

**Brake Fluid:**
- DOT 4 standard
- DOT 4 Low Viscosity (BMW, VW)
- **Never use DOT 3** on European cars

**Differential/Transfer Case:**
- Follow OEM spec (75W-90, 75W-140, etc.)
- Synthetic only

## Repair Philosophy

### **Fix It Right, Not Cheap**

**Our priority:**
1. Safety
2. Reliability
3. Cost

**This means:**
- Recommend proper fix over temporary patch
- Explain both options to customer (proper vs temporary)
- Don't cut corners to save money
- If customer chooses cheap option, document it

**Example:**
- Customer has worn ball joint
- **Proper fix:** Replace control arm with ball joint (~$250)
- **Cheap option:** Press in new ball joint (~$150)
- **Our recommendation:** Replace control arm (more reliable, saves labor if it fails)

---

### **Recommend Related Maintenance**

**When doing a job, check related components:**

**Examples:**
- Timing belt → Recommend water pump, tensioner, seals
- Brake pads → Measure rotors, recommend if thin
- Suspension work → Recommend alignment after
- Coolant leak → Check whole cooling system

**Don't oversell, but don't undersell:**
- Explain what we found
- Explain consequences of not fixing
- Let customer decide
- Document customer decline in writing

---

### **Use Proper Tools**

**Never:**
- Use wrong tool because "it works"
- Skip steps to save time
- Reuse stretch bolts, crush washers, one-time fasteners
- Over-torque plastic components
- Guess at torque specs

**Always:**
- Use torque wrench for critical fasteners
- Use proper specialty tools
- Follow manufacturer procedures
- Replace single-use hardware
- Clean mating surfaces before assembly

---

## Diagnostic Standards

### **Diagnose Before Replacing Parts**

**Never:**
- Throw parts at a problem
- Replace components without testing
- Guess based on symptoms alone

**Always:**
- Test to confirm failure
- Follow logical diagnostic path
- Start with most likely cause (but verify)
- Use scan tool, multimeter, pressure gauges as needed

**Example:**
- P0420 code (cat efficiency)
- **Wrong:** Replace cat immediately
- **Right:** Test O2 sensors, check for exhaust leaks, verify cat is actually bad

---

### **Check for TSBs and Recalls**

**Before diagnosing common issues:**
1. Look up vehicle VIN for open recalls
2. Check for Technical Service Bulletins (TSBs)
3. Search for known issues on this make/model

**Many "weird problems" have factory TSBs with solutions.**

**Resources:**
- NHTSA recall database (free)
- Manufacturer TSB databases
- ALLDATA, Mitchell (subscription)

---

## Customer Communication

### **Set Realistic Expectations**

**Time estimates:**
- Give realistic time, not best-case scenario
- "Should be done by end of day" not "30 minutes"
- Add buffer for unexpected issues

**Cost estimates:**
- Provide range, not single number
- "Parts are $200-300, labor is 3-4 hours at $120/hr, total estimate $560-780"
- Call customer if cost will exceed estimate

**Repair complexity:**
- Explain if diagnosis is needed before estimate
- Explain if we might find additional issues
- Get approval before doing extra work

---

### **Explain in Plain Language**

**Avoid:**
- Pure technician speak ("Your VANOS solenoid is clogged")
- Confusing terms without explanation
- Talking down to customer

**Use:**
- Simple explanations with car terms in parentheses
- "The valve timing system (called VANOS) has a clogged filter"
- Analogies when helpful
- Show customer the failed part

---

### **Document Everything**

**On every repair order:**
- Customer complaint (in their words)
- What we found
- What we recommended
- What customer approved
- What customer declined (get signature)
- Parts used (brand, part numbers)
- Labor time
- Test results (mileage, codes, measurements)

**Photos:**
- Take photos of major issues
- Show customer what we found
- Keep in file for records

## Specific Vehicle Defaults

### **European Vehicles (BMW, Audi, VW, Mercedes)**

**Defaults:**
- Use OEM or OEM-supplier parts (Bosch, Mann, Mahle, Hengst)
- Synthetic oil only, OEM spec required
- Check for coding/adaptation requirements before replacing parts
- Expect electrical issues after battery disconnect (radio, windows, etc.)

**Common needs:**
- Battery registration after replacement (BMW, Audi)
- Throttle body adaptation after cleaning
- Brake pad reset with scan tool

**Warn customer about:**
- Higher parts cost than domestic/Asian
- Possible need for dealer coding ($100-300)
- Diagnostic software requirements

---

### **Japanese Vehicles (Honda, Toyota, Nissan)**

**Defaults:**
- Quality aftermarket acceptable for most parts
- OEM fluids recommended but not critical (except Honda ATF)
- Generally reliable, focus on maintenance items

**Common issues:**
- Honda: Check for TSBs on transmission issues (use OEM ATF only)
- Toyota: Timing belt intervals critical (60-90k miles)
- Nissan: CVT transmissions sensitive to fluid (OEM only)

---

### **Domestic Vehicles (Ford, GM, Chrysler)**

**Defaults:**
- OEM or quality aftermarket acceptable
- GM: Use Dexos oil, don't skip it
- Ford: Use Motorcraft filters and fluids when possible

**Common issues:**
- Electrical gremlins (check grounds, connectors)
- Transmission issues (check fluid level HOT, running, in gear)
- Rust on older vehicles (Midwest)

## Quality Control

### **Every Job Gets:**

1. **Pre-delivery inspection**
   - Test drive if drivability related
   - Verify repair fixed the issue
   - Check for leaks
   - Verify no new warning lights

2. **Clean vehicle**
   - Remove tools from car
   - Wipe down dirty areas we touched
   - Floor mat back in place
   - Seat/mirror back to position (if we moved it)

3. **Documentation**
   - Completed repair order
   - Codes cleared (if applicable)
   - Reset service lights if we did service
   - Any additional notes for customer

## Shop Supplies Standard Charge

**Default: 10-15% of labor cost**

**Covers:**
- Shop rags
- Brake cleaner, contact cleaner, etc.
- Gloves
- Small consumables (zip ties, hose clamps, etc.)

**Does NOT cover:**
- Major fluids (oil, coolant, etc.) - charged separately
- Parts - charged separately

---

## Warranty Policy

**Our warranty on labor:**
- 12 months / 12,000 miles on labor

**Parts warranty:**
- Per parts manufacturer (typically 12-36 months)
- Keep receipts

**Not covered:**
- Customer-supplied parts (labor only, no warranty)
- Wear items (brake pads, wiper blades) if driven hard
- Damage from abuse or neglect
- Related issues that develop later (must be directly caused by our work)

**If warranty claim:**
- Bring vehicle back immediately
- We diagnose to confirm it's related to our work
- If our fault: Free repair
- If new issue: Explain and provide estimate
